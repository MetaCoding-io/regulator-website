Architecture

VSM-Pi

A coding-agent harness built on Pi, with a typed VSM control plane layered on top of it — designed against one recurring failure mode of autonomous agents: architectural drift that outruns anyone's ability to review it.

Design goals

  • Keep the orchestrator small — the S3 loop of contract → dispatch → verify → route → close, generic over .
  • Declare the control plane rather than assemble it: , capability profiles, policy, and workload live in a versioned, checked definition.
  • Make S5 identity and policy durable and version-controlled, independent of any one context window.
  • Keep independent of S1 self-reporting.
  • Differentiate operations by , not roleplay persona.

What VSM is, and why it matters here

Stafford Beer's Viable System Model comes out of management cybernetics, not software: it asks what any system — an organism, a company, an autonomous agent — needs structurally to keep operating as its environment keeps generating more variety than it can be made to predict. Beer's answer is five functions plus an independent audit: operations that touch the world (S1), coordination that keeps them from colliding (S2), control that decides what happens next (S3), intelligence that watches the environment and the future (S4), and identity that says what the system is and isn't allowed to become (S5) — checked by a function that never just takes the operators' word for it (S3*).

Why this matters specifically for an agent harness: the model is not a deterministic component. It changes across versions, has no memory of last quarter's incident, and treats a prompt as advice it can — and under pressure, will — ignore. A harness that regulates entirely through prompt text has exactly as much regulatory variety as the words in that prompt, which is provably less than the variety of a real codebase, a real filesystem, or real money moving. VSM doesn't remove the model's judgment; it gives that judgment a boundary it can't talk its way out of.

Six functions, one loop

Beer's Viable System Model, read as a harness architecture. Regulatory variety flows down as attenuated instruction and back up as amplified evidence — S3* audits independently of what S1 reports, and S4 argues with S3 about the future on S5's terms.

S1 units act directly on the environment; S3* audits independently of what they report; S4 and S3 argue about now vs. next, arbitrated by S5 policy.

Mechanism hierarchy

Prefer the mechanism that survives adversarial pressure

When adding a feature to the harness, VSM-Pi walks this ladder from the top down — reach for the highest level that can actually do the job, and only fall further down when it can't. If you can write the check, don't write the paragraph.

1
TypeScript invariant
Shape the data so the wrong value has nowhere to go — a fixed set of choices, not a free string anyone could mistype. The compiler refuses it before the code ever runs.
2
Deterministic runtime check
A rule enforced by code, every single time, whatever the prompt says — not a sentence someone has to remember to follow.
3
Structured tool / protocol
A fixed, validated shape for a request — no room to phrase around it, the way free text always leaves room.
4
Model judgment
Reached for only when the answer genuinely needs interpretation — there's no formula for which category a merchant belongs to.
5
Prompt / context engineering
Advice for what nothing above can know: house style, taste, a convention that costs little if it's ignored.
The orchestrator

VSM-Pi provides its own orchestrator

The project does not scope itself as a regulation layer riding on GSD-Pi's workflow kernel. GSD-Pi is mid-cutover to a database-authoritative lifecycle, OpenGSD is fanning out into separate projects, and the course lab already proved the regulation layer needs an orchestrator, not GSD's — ten checkpoints of gates, effects, profiles, leases, contracts, budgets, recovery, audit and authority run on bare Pi. packages/ has no GSD dependency; GSD-Pi stays in the course purely as comparison material. How a unit runs through the loop is drawn out on its own page.

Status

The loop runs; the metasystem is next

Four things are true today, each running end to end rather than sketched.

The loop runs. Contract, dispatch, verify, route, close — with , budgets, a versioned recovery policy, and a that trusts host-run evidence, never a unit's own claim about itself. An identity set is rendered from the files, write-protected, and checked at closeout against the branch.
The path is in. A unit can ask a person through one typed tool; only a nonblocking recap continues without an answer. An unanswered question pauses the unit by gate; what's owed to a person is delivered and reminded; who may answer is declared and checked.
The regulators owe evidence now. An eval harness runs the drift scenario under declared arms with outcome and trajectory graders. Three committed reports say where the gated arm loses. Every record carries an switch and a retirement condition, and every instance store reads as OpenTelemetry spans.
And it operates. The lab installs as a Pi package; regulator init installs it into an existing repository under a declared manifest; doctor checks the instance before the first unit and in CI; the base is re-verified after every merge and a failure holds the whole instance; refused words are linted out; the outbox is watched; an identity decision promotes back into the definition.

What remains is the capstone. course/lab is the reference build; each checkpoint promotes into packages/ as it stabilizes. What the build still owes is listed in docs/DEBT.md.

The control room

A projection of registry ∪ execution store ∪ regulatory state. It owns no state of its own — every screen is read from records that already exist elsewhere. packages/control-room serves this page today, over the built by regulator status.

Topology — regulators by function, with the channels each one declares it consumes and emits.
Instances — every instance's units with budget, routing decision, audit verdict and what's owed on them; ; by consumer, with the veto marked; unrouted signals; the instance manifest.
Assurance & lifecycle — the committed eval reports, plus each regulator's review date, ablation switch and retirement condition.
Inspector — the whole record for a regulator or a unit, including the unit's replay in time order.

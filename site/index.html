Why cybernetics

Agents need a regulator, not just a good prompt

The Viable System Model (VSM) is Stafford Beer's answer to an old question: what does any system — an organism, a company, an autonomous agent — need structurally to stay viable while its environment keeps generating more variety than anyone predicted? Ashby's Law gives the constraint: only variety can absorb variety, so a regulator with fewer possible responses than the disturbances it faces will eventually fail somewhere it can't see. A model plus a good prompt is not a regulator — it's a hope, and hope doesn't survive contact with a real codebase, a real filesystem, or real money moving. This course treats the harness itself as the regulator: explicit functions, typed channels, and gates that hold even when the prompt is ignored, the model changes, or the task has nothing to do with code. Who it's for

Not a prompt pack, not a persona library

If you've used a coding agent for real work and hit its limits, this course gives you the vocabulary and the mechanism to fix that — not another framework to bolt on top.

WhoWhat this course gives you
Senior / staff engineersrunning agents on real codebases — a principled reason for every guardrail, tuned to their repo.
Platform & DevEx teamsa design method for an internal agent platform, plus the evaluation machinery to prove it helps.
Agent-tool buildersdeep Pi extension/SDK competence and a structural critique of "five agents in a trench coat."
Technical leadersa vocabulary for where autonomy is safe, and what evidence would demonstrate it.

By the end, you can

  1. Describe a coding-agent harness as a control system: what regulates what, over which channel, with what authority, closing which feedback loop.
  2. Apply the mechanism hierarchy instead of solving every problem with prompt text.
  3. Extend Pi confidently — typed tools, the tool-call lifecycle, context shaping, per-phase model routing, SDK/RPC, packaging.
  4. Design work units as — each decision marked fixed (not up for grabs), delegated (the agent chooses, within bounds), or unresolved (nobody's decided yet) — rather than prose task descriptions.
  5. Build audit that does not depend on the executor's self-report.
  6. Keep system identity durable and version-controlled instead of trapped in a context window.
  7. Decide when a human must be interrupted, and build the channel that does it.
  8. Measure whether your regulation actually improved outcomes, with a control arm.

Work contracts: what you are actually authorizing
What am I actually authorizing this run to decide?

07
Context and budget as regulated resources
What do I spend, and on what?
Turn tokens and turns into a metered, policy-governed resource: route cheap models to cheap phases, compact context without silently discarding a decision that mattered, and cut a run off before it runs away from you.

Failure, recovery, and the retry lattice
What happens on attempt two, and on attempt six?

PART 4S3* — Audit
09
Evidence, not claims
How do I know the work is done?
Build the closeout gate that never takes a unit's word for its own success. It re-runs the checks itself, against the exact commit, and produces a technical verdict that no amount of confident prose in the report can talk its way past — and judges the unit by the test suite it inherited, so a failing test can't be made to pass by deleting it.
tool_call / tool_result hooks · host-run checks · the closeout gate · inherited-tests
Written
10
Authority boundaries and protected state
What must the agent never be able to change?
Draw a hard line around the files an agent must never edit on its own — identity, policy, invariants — and enforce it with a write preflight the agent literally cannot route around, instead of a rule it's merely asked to remember.
tool_call blocking · the write preflight · project trust · BOUNDARY.md
Written
PART 5S4, S5 & the algedonic channel
11
Environmental intelligence and the obligation lifecycle
How does the system learn about its world, and how does that knowledge get a say without becoming the decision?
Add a research unit that can raise a flag about the environment — a dependency advisory, a deadline coming due — and route it into an obligation that stays visible until a person or S3 actually resolves it. Intelligence gets a voice here, never a vote.

Written
12
Durable identity and policy
Six months and four model versions later, what keeps this system the same system?
Move your harness's identity — its invariants, its policy, its domain model — out of any one session's context window and into version-controlled files that only a single, declared decision path is ever allowed to write.

Written
13
Algedonic channels and human interaction contracts
Normal regulation is not working and the loop cannot close. Who gets interrupted, how fast, and what counts as an answer?
Build the exception channel Beer called algedonic — literally "pain/pleasure" — the interrupt that skips the normal chain of command, plus the interaction contract that decides whether silence, a timeout, or a stray click ever counts as a real answer.
ask_human · interaction kinds · pause gate · outbox delivery · disposition authority
Written
PART 6Proving it and shipping it
14
Observability, evals, and longitudinal drift
You added all this regulation. Did it help, or does it just feel rigorous?
Run your harness against a scripted drift scenario with an ablation arm — the same system with one regulator switched off — and walk away with a statistically-backed answer about what your guardrails actually bought you, not a feeling.

Written
15
Packaging and operating your harness
It works on your laptop. How does your team run it on Monday, and who owns it in three months?
Ship the harness as an installable package with a CI entry point, a doctor command, and a review date on every regulator in the registry — so "who owns this, and is it still earning its keep" has a file behind it, not just a hallway answer.

Written
CAPSTONE — THE VIABILITY CASE

Present the finished harness as a control system, not a feature demo: a system map, a channel table, a mechanism ledger, an enforcement-boundary statement, evidence, the residual uncertainty that's still unresolved, and a boundary critique — who may amend the system's purpose, who is affected but cannot challenge it, what its metrics conceal, and when the system itself should be retired. The harness you present has to have survived one run in which the regulatory machinery itself failed, not only its executor; and the crit attacks from the course's catalog of pathologies, the ways a viable system fails as a whole.
